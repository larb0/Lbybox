Light response

DSP offline

These controls change how the lights react to music; they do not change the sound. Lower a scale if that band keeps the lights at full intensity.

0.55

Sensitivity to bass and kick drums. Higher makes bass-driven lighting stronger; lower stops it staying at full intensity.

0.75

Sensitivity to vocals, guitars and other midrange sounds. Higher gives this band more influence on reactive lighting.

0.90

Sensitivity to cymbals and other high-frequency sounds. Higher makes treble-driven lighting more pronounced.

0.0060

Minimum audio level used by the light analyser. Raising it reduces reactions to quiet hiss; lowering it lets very quiet music affect the lights.

6000 ms

How long the analyser remembers recent loud passages, in milliseconds. Higher adapts more slowly; lower follows level changes more quickly and can make the lights fluctuate more.

220 Hz

Highest frequency the lights count as bass, in hertz. Lower makes Bass Pulse, Wave and Strobe focus on kick drums and sub-bass; higher lets bass guitar and low vocals drive them too. Affects the lights only, never the sound.

Light behaviour

Fine-tune the current six effects. Every control below maps to behaviour that is active in this firmware.

0.55

How quickly reactive lighting rises on a beat. Higher values react faster; lower values soften the start of each pulse.

0.07

How quickly reactive lighting falls after a beat. Lower values create a longer, smoother fade; higher values drop faster.

1.00

Multiplier for the wait between Sparkle glints. Lower creates more frequent sparkles; higher leaves more black space between them.

22

How much sparkle brightness is removed each frame. Higher makes sparkles disappear sooner; lower leaves longer trails.

70 ms

Minimum waiting time before Strobe can retrigger on another bass hit. Keep this below the maximum.

420 ms

Upper end of the retrigger wait used by Strobe. A larger maximum spaces flash trains further apart. Keep this above the minimum.

205

Steady base brightness for Colour Flow. Bass glints sit on top of this.

0.48

Bass level needed to throw a white glint. Lower reacts more often.

0.43

Bass level needed to launch a front-to-back wave.

620 ms

Time for the wave wall to travel from the front port to the deepest row.

54

Depth width of the moving wave. Lower is a sharper wall; higher is broader.

0.50

Bass level needed to start a full-brightness flash train.

22 ms

Length of each individual full-brightness flash.

34 ms

Dark gap between flashes inside a double/triple flash train.

55 ms

Milliseconds per LED before the music-driven midrange speed boost. Lower is faster.

11

Base trail length in LEDs.

18

Extra trail length available on bass hits. Zero keeps tail length fixed.

0.58

Bass level needed to throw the small extra sparkle cluster.

3000 ms

How long without detected audio before reactive effects switch to their calm idle behaviour, in milliseconds. Higher avoids switching during pauses between songs. Music resuming switches back straight away.

35%

Peak brightness of the calm idle behaviour while nothing is playing, as a percentage of full brightness. Colour Flow and Comet keep moving at reduced intensity.

Compressor

DSP offline
-18.0 dB

Bass level where compression begins, in digital dB. More negative values start reducing bass earlier; values closer to zero allow stronger bass peaks.

3.0

Strength of bass compression above its threshold. 1 means no compression; 3 means a 3 dB increase above the threshold becomes about 1 dB.

-15.0 dB

Midrange level where compression begins. More negative values act earlier on vocals and instruments; values closer to zero allow more variation.

2.5

Strength of midrange compression above its threshold. Higher makes loud midrange passages more controlled; 1 leaves them uncompressed.

-12.0 dB

Treble level where compression begins. More negative values act earlier on cymbals and other bright sounds.

2.0

Strength of treble compression above its threshold. Higher reduces loud high-frequency peaks more strongly; 1 means no compression.

Battery warnings

Bluetooth board offline

Voltages, not percentages — a flat LiFePO4 curve makes percentage an interpretation, while volts are a measurement. The labels are roughly where each sits on the curve. Warnings play even when the speaker is muted and use the averaged voltage shown on the main page.

5 min

Battery percentage and warning chimes use this rolling average to ignore music-related sag. Live voltage is still shown separately, and the emergency mute still watches live voltage.

12.60 V

Loudness contour

DSP offline

The ear hears less bass at low levels, so quiet music sounds thin. This lifts bass as volume comes down and removes it as volume rises — which is also where excursion matters most. Set the boost to 0 to turn it off entirely.

7.0 dB

Extra low-bass boost at very low volume, on top of the selected EQ profile. It decreases as volume rises. Zero requests no extra contour boost.

75%

Volume level where the extra loudness boost fades to zero. Higher keeps the boost active further up the volume range. Your normal EQ profile still applies.

Sustained-level backoff & limiter

DSP offline
0.45

Sustained signal level above which the RMS stage gradually reduces overall gain. Lower starts backoff sooner. This is a signal estimate, not a temperature measurement.

0.30

Signal level below which reduced gain starts recovering. Lower waits for quieter audio before recovery. Keep it below the backoff threshold.

1.00

Lowest gain the RMS stage can approach. 1.00 disables new gain reduction from startup; 0.75 retains 75% of signal amplitude at maximum backoff. Lower allows more reduction.

0.5 ms

How quickly the limiter detector responds to peaks, in milliseconds. Smaller values react faster; larger values allow longer transients before this stage reacts.

120 ms

How quickly the limiter detector settles after a peak. Larger values recover more slowly; smaller values recover faster but can make level changes more noticeable.
