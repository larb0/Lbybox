Light response

DSP offline

These controls change how the lights react to music; they do not change the sound. Lower a scale if that band keeps the lights at full intensity.

0.55

Sensitivity to bass and kick drums. Higher makes bass-driven lighting stronger; lower stops it staying at full intensity.

0.75

Sensitivity to vocals, guitars and other midrange sounds. Higher gives this band more influence on reactive lighting.

0.90

Sensitivity to cymbals and other high-frequency sounds. Higher makes treble-driven lighting more pronounced.

0.0060

Minimum audio level used by the light analyser. Raising it reduces reactions to quiet hiss; lowering it lets very quiet music affect the lights.

6000 ms

How long the analyser remembers recent loud passages, in milliseconds. Higher adapts more slowly; lower follows level changes more quickly and can make the lights fluctuate more.

220 Hz

Highest frequency counted as bass for Comet tail, Colour Flow glints and Strobe impacts. Bass Pulse has its own band below so changing this does not alter its pulse character.

55 Hz

Lowest frequency allowed to drive Bass Pulse. Raise it if deep sustained sub-bass keeps the effect glowing between kicks.

180 Hz

Highest frequency allowed to drive Bass Pulse. Lower values focus more tightly on kick/sub-bass; higher values include more bass guitar and low mids.

Light behaviour

Fine-tune the current six effects. Every control below maps to behaviour that is active in this firmware.

0.55

How quickly reactive lighting rises on a beat. Higher values react faster; lower values soften the start of each pulse.

0.07

How quickly reactive lighting falls after a beat. Lower values create a longer, smoother fade; higher values drop faster.

1.00

Main Sparkle speed control: lower = more frequent glints, higher = more black time. Treble also speeds spawning up while music plays; Sparkle decay controls how long each glint remains visible.

22

How much sparkle brightness is removed each frame. Higher makes sparkles disappear sooner; lower leaves longer trails.

205

Steady base brightness for Colour Flow. Music adds white glints without pumping this background.

0.36

How easily bass adds extra white glints. Lower reacts to smaller hits.

1.45

Overall white-glint density from music. Higher adds more sparkle while leaving the colour flow itself steady.

1050 ms

Time for the wave to travel from the back of the port to the front. It then reverses and travels back continuously.

58

Depth width of the moving wave. Lower is a thin crest; higher makes a broad rolling wave.

22%

How much music nudges wave brightness and colour. Zero is a pure back-and-forth animation; higher is more reactive.

5.0 Hz

Base flash rate during calmer music. The strobe speeds up automatically as the track gets more energetic.

11.0 Hz

Maximum ordinary flash rate during high-energy music. Bass impacts still add their own immediate double-hit.

0.50

Bass level needed to punch an immediate white + colour double flash over the normal strobe.

20 ms

Length of each full-brightness flash.

32 ms

Black gap between the white and colour flashes in a bass impact.

55 ms

Milliseconds per LED. Lower is faster. Music no longer changes travel speed; bass changes tail/flare instead.

11

Base trail length in LEDs.

18

Extra trail length available on bass hits. This plus the white head flare keeps Comet music-reactive without changing its speed.

0.12

0.22

How quickly Bass Pulse falls after a hit. Higher gives shorter, punchier pulses.

5

Minimum brightness while music is active but bass is below the pulse gate. Lower gives more black between hits.

0.62

How big a kick has to be to switch Bass Pulse to a new colour or colour combination. Lower changes colour more often; higher saves it for the biggest hits.

0.58

Bass level needed to throw the small extra sparkle cluster.

3000 ms

How long without detected audio before reactive effects switch to their calm idle behaviour, in milliseconds. Higher avoids switching during pauses between songs. Music resuming switches back straight away.

35%

Peak brightness of the calm idle behaviour while nothing is playing, as a percentage of full brightness. Colour Flow and Comet keep moving at reduced intensity.

Battery warnings

Bluetooth board offline

Voltages, not percentages — a flat LiFePO4 curve makes percentage an interpretation, while volts are a measurement. The labels are roughly where each sits on the curve. Warnings play even when the speaker is muted and use the averaged voltage shown on the main page.

5 min

Battery percentage and warning chimes use this rolling average to ignore music-related sag. Live voltage is still shown separately, and the emergency mute still watches live voltage.

12.95 V

One long beep, once.

12.75 V

Two long beeps, once.

12.00 V

Repeats every 8 seconds until charged.

12.00 V

Audio stops here. Normally the same as the final warning.

12.60 V

Must be at least 0.15 V above the cutoff to prevent rapid on/off switching.

--

The resting voltage your pack reads at each percentage. The battery percentage is read off this curve, with straight lines between points, using the averaged voltage. Set the points from a few measured rest voltages for your pack.

s
V

Average is how many seconds of voltage the percentage is averaged over; the warnings above keep their own window. LED fix adds volts back for the LED grid's own current draw, estimated by the LED board and smoothed over a couple of seconds, then averaged again over the window above. The amount is what the LEDs cost at about 2 A of LED current (a bright, mostly lit grid); dimmer lighting adds proportionally less. 0 ignores the LEDs.

Volts added back to the percentage voltage while the speaker is playing. Each box is the correction at that average output level, in dBFS (the level the DSP reports, shown live above, so you can see where your music sits; closer to 0 is louder). Higher levels never get a smaller correction, so changing one box moves its neighbours with it. All zero = off. To tune it, play steadily at a few levels and raise the matching point until the percentage stops dropping.

Loudness contour

DSP offline

The ear hears less bass at low levels, so quiet music sounds thin. This lifts bass as volume comes down and removes it as volume rises — which is also where excursion matters most. Set the boost to 0 to turn it off entirely.

7.0 dB

Extra low-bass boost at very low volume, on top of the selected EQ profile. It decreases as volume rises. Zero requests no extra contour boost.

75%

Volume level where the extra loudness boost fades to zero. Higher keeps the boost active further up the volume range. Your normal EQ profile still applies.
